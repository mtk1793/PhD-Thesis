# CAPSM academic thesis writing guide — 2026-11-07

This is a focused editorial companion to the CAPSM research manuscript, not a study of 50 dissertations. It synthesises verified public university guidance from MIT, Stanford, Oxford, Cambridge, and ETH Zurich. Bibliographic metadata is available in `references/academic_writing_guidance.bib`.

## Structure and chapter argument
- Lead with the research problem and scope; connect each chapter's contribution to the thesis objectives.
- Use the progression: transmission-system challenge → literature synthesis → research gap → CAPSM architecture → implementation → reproducible experimental design → results → critical discussion → conclusions.
- Open each chapter with a purpose statement, and close with a substantive transition to the next chapter.

## Literature synthesis
- Compare published controllers by control objective, physical representation, network constraints, data assumptions, computational burden, operating timescale, and type of validation.
- Avoid universal gap claims: a documented search may support 'no directly comparable system was identified', not 'no such method exists'.

## Argumentation and prose
- Make one main claim per paragraph, then explain the engineering mechanism, cite evidence, and state the implication.
- Use explicit logical transitions, not successive 'Moreover' or 'Furthermore' clauses.
- Prefer short declarative sentences. Define acronyms, variables, datasets and constraints at first use.
- Use 'formulates', 'implements', 'evaluates', 'measures', and 'demonstrates' only when those statements match verifiable evidence.

## Mathematical clarity and claim boundaries
- The CAPSM shared action interface has nine positions, eight physically active; index 5 is reserved.
- System 2 uses 32 one-step quantum-inspired candidates; there is no demonstrated quantum advantage or multistep planning.
- Convexity of the action box does **not** establish AC-network feasibility, dynamic security, or closed-loop stability.
- PSS/E shunt/series equivalents are AC steady-state proxies, not dynamic converter models.
- Distinguish historical PYPOWER Stage-1 data, independently evidenced HIL records, design targets, and new licensed PSS/E runs.

## Methodology and discussion
- Record the case source and hash, PSS/E minor version, RAW import and solver settings, data ranges, train/validation/test splits, model checkpoints, random seeds, nonconvergence rules and matched baselines.
- Discussion interprets verified evidence, acknowledges limitations, and distinguishes system-specific findings from generalisation.

## Phrase examples
**Weak:** 'CAPSM guarantees optimal power-system security.'  
**Stronger:** 'CAPSM restricts candidate setpoints to specified equipment bounds; network-voltage and thermal constraints require separate verification.'

**Weak:** 'QIRL runs 32 quantum solutions in parallel.'  
**Stronger:** 'System 2 evaluates 32 one-step candidate actions using a quantum-inspired selection rule. Runtime and relative efficiency remain experimental questions.'

**Weak:** 'The German data reproduce the IEEE 39-bus system.'  
**Stronger:** 'OPSD German hourly profiles supply measured temporal patterns, mapped synthetically onto the 60 Hz IEEE 39-bus benchmark.'

**Weak:** 'HIL proves the controller is ready for field deployment.'  
**Stronger:** 'Controller-HIL tests can substantiate timing behaviour within their tested hardware and model conditions; field readiness requires additional protection and equipment validation.'
