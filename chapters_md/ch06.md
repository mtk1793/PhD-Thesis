# Chapter 6: Stability Enhancement Using Deep Learning

Stability assessment is the second pillar of the deep-learning layer of CAPSM. Chapter 4 established the CNN-LSTM encoder that turns raw operating observations into control actions; this chapter re-uses the same architectural machinery for a different question, namely whether the system is stable, how far it is from instability, and which events justify reflex action. The chapter reviews the classical assessment approaches, specifies the CNN-LSTM stability assessor, treats voltage stability and its measurement through the loading margin, introduces the autoencoder anomaly score, and explains how the stability module feeds the System 1 reflex path of the CAPSM architecture. As throughout the thesis, design targets are labelled as such and measured results are attributed to the Stage-1 rebuild or the HIL campaign.

## 6.1 Transient Stability Assessment Approaches

Transient stability concerns the ability of the synchronous machines to remain in synchronism after a severe disturbance such as a short circuit or the loss of a heavily loaded circuit [36]. Three families of assessment methods coexist. Direct methods construct a transient energy function and compare the energy injected by the disturbance against a critical energy threshold; the classical practical method of Athay, Podmore, and Virmani made this approach computable for realistic systems and remains the reference formulation of the transient energy function [43]. Time-domain simulation integrates the full differential-algebraic model through the disturbance and is the most faithful method, but its cost makes it impractical to run inside a real-time control loop for every candidate contingency. Machine-learning assessment builds a fast surrogate that maps measured features to a stability verdict, and modern implementations reach assessment speeds that time-domain integration cannot match [87], [88].

The canonical severity metric for transient stability is the critical clearing time (CCT), defined in Equation (6.1) as the largest fault-clearing time for which the post-fault trajectory remains transiently stable, where S(t_cl) is the indicator of transient stability for a fault cleared at time t_cl. A controller that can estimate the CCT margin in real time gains a direct measure of how much response delay the system tolerates, which is precisely the quantity the reflex layer must respect.

```eq
%%6.1
t_{\mathrm{CCT}} = \sup \left\{ t_{\mathrm{cl}} \,:\, S(t_{\mathrm{cl}}) = 1 \right\}
```

Complementary to the transient view, small-signal assessment characterises the decay of oscillation modes through the eigenvalues of the linearised system. For an oscillatory eigenvalue pair the damping ratio is defined in Equation (6.2), where zeta is the damping ratio, omega_n the natural frequency, and omega_d the damped frequency [123], [124]. A damping ratio below conventionally accepted thresholds signals poorly damped inter-area or local modes, and it is this quantity that synthetic inertia and V2G damping services aim to improve, as discussed in Section 6.4.

```eq
%%6.2
\lambda_d = -\zeta \omega_n \pm j \omega_d, \quad \omega_d = \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}, \quad \zeta = \frac{-\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_d)}{|\lambda_d|}
```

The assessment philosophy of CAPSM follows from the dual-process architecture. Direct methods and time-domain simulation are too slow or too scenario-specific for closed-loop use, so they serve offline as sources of training labels and validation cases. The learned assessor, described next, provides the fast inner loop, and its verdicts are treated as advisory signals that trigger reflex actions rather than as unqualified truth. This division of labour keeps the mathematical rigour of the classical methods where it is affordable and the speed of learning where it is necessary.

## 6.2 CNN-LSTM Architecture for Real-Time Stability Assessment

The stability assessor re-uses the System 1 encoder without structural modification. The input at each step is the same 160-feature observation vector defined in Chapter 4, comprising bus voltage magnitudes and angles, branch flows, generator outputs, FACTS setpoints, EV powers, and scalar system quantities, and the sliding window retains seq_len = 12 consecutive hourly observations. The trunk proceeds from the two Conv1d layers (1 to 64 and 64 to 64 channels, kernel 3) through adaptive average pooling into the LSTM with hidden size 128, the tanh-attention pooling over time, and the fully connected head, giving approximately 250K parameters in total; the full derivation appears in Chapter 4. For stability assessment the output head is reinterpreted: instead of the nine-dimensional action vector, the head emits a stability index from which the event classifier of Section 6.5 derives the classes fault, oscillation, and instability. Figure 6.2 shows the architecture with the assessment head.

![Figure 6.2: CNN-LSTM architecture for real-time stability assessment, re-using the System 1 encoder.](figures/fig_cnnlstm_arch.png)

The attention mechanism deserves a remark because it is what turns a window of 12 observations into a decision. The attention weights are computed as in Equation (6.3), where h_j is the LSTM hidden state at window position j, and the context vector aggregates the hidden states with weights that emphasise the time steps most relevant to the current assessment. Intuitively, the network can attend to the ramp onset or the voltage dip inside the window rather than averaging it away, which matters when the discriminating information is concentrated in two or three of the twelve steps. The training procedure is the behaviour-cloning setup of Chapter 4, so no new training machinery is introduced for the stability head.

```eq
%%6.3
c_t = \sum_{j=1}^{T} a_j h_j, \quad a_j = \frac{\exp(e_j)}{\sum_{k} \exp(e_k)}, \quad e_j = v^T \tanh(W_a h_j)
```

The principal known weakness of purely data-driven stability assessment is its dependence on the coverage of the training scenarios, which motivates the physics-informed variant. A physics-informed neural network (PINN) augments the data loss with a penalty on the residual of the physical model, as expressed in Equation (6.4), where r_theta denotes the residual of the governing swing or power-flow relations evaluated on the network output [31]. In the CAPSM framework specification the PINN-assisted stability prediction is set at 96.8 percent accuracy against 84.2 percent for the purely data-driven baseline, with a 73 percent reduction in required training data; we report these figures strictly as design targets from the framework specification, not as Stage-1 measurements. The mechanism, however, is well understood: penalising physical residuals constrains the function class to trajectories compatible with the power system dynamics, so generalisation improves most where data is sparsest [31].

```eq
%%6.4
L = L_{\mathrm{data}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}\, ||r_{\theta}||^2
```

For deployment, the assessor runs on the same inference path as the System 1 controller, so its cost profile is the already-measured one: a full evaluation step takes 32.0 ms on CPU in the Stage-1 rebuild, and the HIL deployment allocates the encoder its own core with a 5 ms reflex task, as detailed in Chapter 10. The assessor therefore adds assessment capability without adding a second model to maintain, which is a deliberate economy of the dual-process design.

## 6.3 Voltage Stability Enhancement Techniques

Voltage stability concerns the ability of the system to maintain acceptable voltages at all buses under normal and contingent operation [36], [37]. The graphical workhorse of quasi-static voltage stability is the PV curve, which traces a monitored bus voltage against the loading scale as the load increases toward the point of power-flow non-existence. Figure 6.1 shows the PV-curve concept with the nose point marking the loadability boundary; the distance from the current operating point to that boundary is the loading margin, the quantity that the CAPSM environment measures directly.

![Figure 6.1: PV curves at a monitored bus with the loadability boundary and loading margin.](figures/fig_pv_curves.png)

Formally, the loading margin is defined in Equation (6.5) as the largest uniform load scaling for which the AC power flow, started from the base operating point (x_0, P_0) with device setpoints and dispatch rules held fixed, still converges. The continuation power flow made the systematic tracing of such curves practical [125], the nose point corresponds to a saddle-node bifurcation of the power flow equations [126], and fast indices approximate the distance to that bifurcation without tracing the full curve [127]. The bisection approach adopted here occupies a middle ground: it does not trace the curve, but it brackets the boundary to arbitrary precision with pure convergence tests, which suits an environment whose only oracle is a Newton power flow.

```eq
%%6.5
\lambda^* = \sup \left\{ \lambda \in [1, \lambda_{\max}] : \mathrm{PF}(x_0, \lambda P_0)\ \mathrm{converges} \right\}
```

The enhancement side of voltage stability rests on reactive reserve management and coordinated FACTS dispatch. Keeping reactive reserves distributed so that the electrically weak regions can be supported locally, and coordinating shunt and series devices so that flows are steered away from stressed corridors, are the two classical levers [36], [37]. In CAPSM these levers appear as the placement decisions of Chapter 5, which fix where support is available, and the dispatch layers of Chapters 4 and 9, which decide how the available support is applied at each step. The stability module contributes the measurement layer: it quantifies the margin so that both placement and dispatch can be judged by a common stability currency.

The implemented margin algorithm in the Stage-1 environment works as follows. The routine searches the load-scale interval lambda in [1, 3], first performing a coarse scan in steps of 0.05 from the base point, retaining the largest scaling that still converges and stopping at the first divergence. It then refines the bracket by eight bisection iterations, halving the interval each time, and finally restores the base operating point by re-solving at the unscaled load while preserving the curtailment bookkeeping, so the margin measurement leaves the environment exactly as it found it. The returned value is the converged endpoint of the final bracket, and its accuracy is the subject of Theorem 6.1.

**Theorem 6.1 (Bisection localisation of the loading margin).** Let the coarse scan with step h = 0.05 produce a bracket (lambda_lo, lambda_hi] with lambda_lo converged and lambda_hi divergent, where lambda_hi - lambda_lo = h. After n = 8 refinement iterations of the implemented bisection, the returned margin lambda_hat satisfies the error bound of Equation (6.6); that is, the true loadability boundary is localised within (lambda_hi - lambda_lo)/2^8, which for h = 0.05 amounts to approximately 1.95 x 10^-4 in load scale.

```eq
%%6.6
|\hat{\lambda} - \lambda^*| \le \frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{hi}} - \lambda_{\mathrm{lo}}}{2^8} = \frac{0.05}{256} \approx 1.95 \times 10^{-4}
```

*Proof.* The invariant maintained by the bisection is that lambda_lo always converges and lambda_hi always diverges, so the boundary lies in the closed bracket [lambda_lo, lambda_hi] (the classical root-bracketing invariant of bisection [109]). Each iteration evaluates the midpoint, keeps the converged endpoint, and replaces the divergent endpoint by the midpoint or keeps it, which halves the bracket width; by induction the width after n iterations is (lambda_hi - lambda_lo)/2^n. The returned value is the converged endpoint of the final bracket, and the boundary differs from some point of that bracket by at most the bracket width, which gives the stated bound. ∎

One assumption is worth surfacing: the theorem localises the boundary detected by convergence testing, under the practical monotonicity that a converged solution at some scaling implies convergence at all smaller scalings within the searched range. This held without exception across the Stage-1 campaign, and the restoration step guarantees the margin measurement has no side effects on the environment. The measured margins are the valley value of 2.53 times the base load at the valley operating point of 12 January, 03:00 (2,388 MW), and the peak value of 1.452 times the base load at the peak operating point of 12 January, 18:00 (6,195 MW), giving a valley-to-peak ratio of 1.74 (all measured in the Stage-1 rebuild). These values define the operating envelope within which the controllers of Chapters 4 and 9 must keep the system, and the framework specification sets a design target of a 28.5 percent loading-margin improvement under coordinated stability enhancement, to be judged against exactly these measured baselines.

## 6.4 Custom ML Models for Grid Stability

Beyond the supervised assessor, the stability module employs an autoencoder trained to reconstruct normal operating observations. The encoder E maps an observation x to a latent code and the decoder D reconstructs it; the anomaly score is the reconstruction error defined in Equation (6.7). Because the autoencoder is trained only on healthy data, it reconstructs familiar operating patterns accurately and unfamiliar ones poorly, so a large reconstruction error flags observations that deviate from anything seen in normal operation [33].

```eq
%%6.7
r(x) = ||x - D(E(x))||^2
```

The alarm threshold is set at the 99.7th percentile of the reconstruction-error distribution over the validation window, which under the approximately Gaussian tail of the validation errors corresponds to the classical three-sigma exclusion and yields a false-alarm budget of roughly three in one thousand evaluations. The score is used in two complementary roles. As a stability score, sustained elevation of the reconstruction error indicates that the system is operating outside the learned normal envelope, which complements the physical margins of Section 6.3 with a learned, distribution-level view. As a detection score, the same statistic feeds the fault-detection stack of Chapter 7, where it catches anomalies that model-based residual tests miss; the chapter presents the formal statement of that complementarity in its Section 7.3.

The stability context of these learned scores is shaped by the changing resource mix. Virtual synchronous generators and synthetic inertia alter the damping characteristics of low-inertia systems, and V2G fleets contribute controllable fast response whose behaviour differs from that of synchronous machines [85], [86]. An assessor trained purely on synchronous-dominant trajectories may misjudge the new dynamics, which is why the autoencoder score is deliberately distribution-level (it flags any departure from the learned normal envelope) and why the framework specification foresees retraining windows as the resource mix evolves. The damping-ratio framework of Equation (6.2) remains the physical yardstick against which such retraining is validated [123], [124].

## 6.5 Integration with the CAPSM System 1 Reflex Path

The stability module closes its loop through the System 1 reflex path. The assessment head, the loading-margin machinery, and the autoencoder score are distilled by the event classifier into three event classes: fault, oscillation, and instability. A fault event (abrupt flow or voltage excursion) triggers immediate reflex action from the pre-trained System 1 policy; an oscillation event triggers damping-oriented setpoint adjustments and flags the Metacognitive Arbiter; an instability event escalates to System 2 deliberation and, if needed, safe-mode behaviour. The reflex actions are budgeted at sub-5 ms in the HIL deployment, where the encoder occupies its own core in the four-core allocation (Chapter 10); the offline Stage-1 implementation performs a full evaluation step, including the encoder forward pass and the environment step, in 32.0 ms on CPU (measured in the Stage-1 rebuild). The arbiter then weighs the reflex response against the deliberative response exactly as specified in Chapter 4.

A design element foreseen by the framework is generative augmentation of the training corpus. An RNN-based trajectory generator is specified to synthesise additional control-signal trajectories for training, expanding the diversity of scenarios that the assessor and the reflex policy see beyond the harvested benchmark windows. We describe this as a design element deliberately: the Stage-1 results reported in this thesis do not depend on generative augmentation, and the mechanism remains an architectural provision whose evaluation belongs to future work. Its rationale, however, is consistent with the transfer-learning discussion of Chapter 5, since both mechanisms address the same scarcity problem from different directions.

The performance expectations for the integrated stability layer are set by the framework specification and are reported here with their proper label. The specification targets a 42 percent stability-metric enhancement in extreme events and a CCT improvement of 20 to 33 percent from the coordinated stability measures. Both figures are design targets, not Stage-1 measurements, and Chapter 11 will account for them against the measured baselines. What the Stage-1 rebuild does establish is the measurement foundation on which such claims must be tested: the loading margins of 2.53 and 1.452 times base load at valley and peak, the 721-step January 2019 benchmark with 100 percent power-flow convergence, and the event-injection machinery that Chapters 7 and 10 reuse.

## 6.6 Chapter Summary

This chapter assembled the stability-assessment layer of CAPSM from three complementary instruments. The CNN-LSTM assessor re-uses the System 1 encoder, its 160-feature observation, its 12-step window, and its attention pooling, with a reinterpreted output head and an optional physics-informed loss whose specified gains (96.8 percent accuracy against 84.2 percent data-driven, and 73 percent data reduction) are design targets. The voltage-stability instrument measures the loading margin by coarse scan plus eight bisection refinements, and Theorem 6.1 established that the implemented procedure localises the loadability boundary within (lambda_hi - lambda_lo)/2^8, about 1.95 x 10^-4 in load scale; the measured margins of 2.53 (valley) and 1.452 (peak) times base load anchor the operating envelope. The autoencoder instrument contributes a distribution-level anomaly score thresholded at the 99.7th percentile, resilient in spirit to the changing damping landscape of low-inertia systems with synthetic inertia and V2G resources [85], [86].

Integration with the System 1 reflex path turns assessment into action: the event classes fault, oscillation, and instability trigger graded responses within the sub-5 ms HIL reflex budget, with the arbiter managing the hand-off to deliberation. The design targets of 42 percent stability enhancement and 20 to 33 percent CCT improvement are stated with their proper label and await the HIL-based accounting of Chapters 10 and 11. The stability layer thus completes the perceptual half of the dual-process loop that Chapter 7 continues on the fault-detection side.
